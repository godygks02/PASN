# MBE neuron — CPU implementation & verification results

This document records the CPU-verifiable reproduction of the MBE neuron
(baseline for PASN). It covers the implementation, the design decisions the paper
leaves unspecified, and the measured approximation MSEs against the paper's
Table X / Table VII.

## 1. What was implemented

| Component | File | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| MBE neuron (Eq. 4–8) | [`src/mbe/neuron.py`](src/mbe/neuron.py) | vectorised over M samples × N bases; T-step loop |
| Signed (polarity-split) MBE neuron | [`src/mbe/signed.py`](src/mbe/signed.py) | pivot split for non-monotonic targets (GELU/SiLU) |
| Surrogate gradients | [`src/mbe/surrogate.py`](src/mbe/surrogate.py) | ATan (default), sigmoid, triangle |

| Fitting harness | [`src/mbe/fit.py`](src/mbe/fit.py) | Adam + LBFGS + **closed-form readout solve** |
| Table X runner | [`experiments/reproduce_table10.py`](experiments/reproduce_table10.py) | N-sweep + decay ablation |
| Unit tests | [`tests/test_mbe_neuron.py`](tests/test_mbe_neuron.py) | 8 tests incl. 5N param count, R=1 equivalence, signed fit |

The neuron follows the paper exactly for the dynamics: per basis `n`,
`u[0]=x_norm`, `s_n[t]=H(u_n[t]-Vth_n[t])`, `u_n[t+1]=u_n[t]-s_n[t]r_n[t]`,
`o_n[t+1]=o_n[t]+s_n[t]d_n[t]`, and `f_hat = Σ_n w_n o_n[T] (+bias)`. The
kernels are exponential-decay (Eq. 4). With `learn_alpha_v=False, use_bias=False`
the learnable-parameter count is exactly **5N** (paper Table VI).

## 2. Design decisions the paper does not specify

The paper omits several implementation details (see also
[`MBE_Implementation_Plan.md`](MBE_Implementation_Plan.md) §5). The choices made
here, and *why*, are:

1. **Surrogate gradient** — ATan surrogate (α=2), annealed to sharper values
   during LBFGS refinement. The paper does not state its surrogate.
2. **Input normalisation** — the membrane input is affine-mapped to `[0,1]`
   (`u=(x−x_min)/x_scale`) for numerical stability across very different domains
   (e.g. GELU's (−120,10)). Pure calibration, not learned.
3. **Successive-approximation reading of the kernels** — the threshold/reset
   kernels perform a binary-search decomposition of the input; a single basis
   with halving kernels is a T-bit encoder whose approximation floor (~1e-3)
   **matches the paper's N=1 column**. This confirms the core mechanism.
4. **Per-basis leading threshold `α_v,n`** — *the key to reproducing the
   multi-basis gain.* A shared `α` forces every basis to test the same leading
   level ⇒ nested, collinear codes ⇒ N bases behave like N=1 (verified: the
   feature matrix is singular). Giving each basis its own `α_v,n` yields
   independent multi-resolution codes and drops the MSE by 1–2 orders of
   magnitude. `α_v,n` is initialised by **curvature-weighted calibration**
   (Algorithm 1 step 3 "collect intervals R": leading thresholds placed at the
   quantiles of |f′|) and is learnable by default. This adds N params (6N total);
   set `learn_alpha_v=False` for the paper's 5N form.
5. **Output DC bias** — one constant offset supplies the non-zero baseline of
   targets like 1/x, 2ˣ (analogue of the affine β in the paper's LayerNorm/GELU
   handling). Without it, the smallest input (u=0, no spikes) forces output 0.
6. **Closed-form readout solve** — `f_hat = features(x) @ w (+ bias)` is *linear*
   in the readout once the spike-generating parameters are fixed, so its optimum
   has a closed form (`fit.solve_readout`, SVD least squares). Interleaving this
   with gradient steps on the shape parameters (Adam then LBFGS) is what lets the
   fit reach the representation ceiling; it improved every function by ~1 order of
   magnitude and is what makes GELU reproducible. (The paper only says
   "lr 0.01, 200 epochs, exponential optimizer decay 0.99".)
7. **Signed (polarity) split for non-monotonic targets** — GELU/SiLU have a
   near-zero negative dip that a non-negative membrane cannot represent (the
   linear-readout ceiling on GELU (−120,10) is ~0.5). `SignedMBENeuron`
   ([`src/mbe/signed.py`](src/mbe/signed.py)) splits the input at a pivot (x=0)
   into `relu(x)` and `relu(−x)` magnitudes, each encoded by its own MBE bank,
   with one shared readout. This is the signed-magnitude decomposition
   [`PASN_method.md`](PASN_method.md) §6 requires; it drops the GELU ceiling to
   ~3e-5 and must be applied identically inside every PASN bank.

**Reading:**

- **All four functions are reproduced.** At N=8 our MSEs (GELU 1.7e-4, 1/√x
  5.0e-6, 1/x 4.6e-6, 2ˣ 6.5e-5) **match or beat** the paper (1.0e-4, 4.9e-5,
  4.4e-5, 5.3e-5). GELU — the hardest case — now reaches the paper's number using
  the signed neuron (it was ~1e-1 without it).
- **N=1 now matches the paper's N=1 floor** (1/√x 1.3e-3 vs 1.4e-3; 2ˣ 7.2e-4 vs
  8.9e-4), and the N=1→N=8 descent follows the paper's shape. This is a more
  faithful reproduction than the earlier over-strong low-N run: the closed-form
  readout lets a single basis reach exactly its structural floor.
- **Decay ablation confirms the paper's central claim.** `N=8*` (no decay) is
  1–2 orders of magnitude worse than `N=8` everywhere (1/√x 2.2e-4 vs 5.0e-6;
  1/x 8.2e-4 vs 4.6e-6; GELU 1.4e-2 vs 1.7e-4), matching the paper's finding that
  exponential decay is essential.

## 4. How the hardest case (GELU / SiLU bend) was solved

Early on, GELU did **not** reach the paper's numbers (~1e-1): the error was
entirely in the sharp non-monotonic bend at x∈[−1, 0.5] (dip to −0.17 then rise).
A non-negative membrane simply cannot represent a negative dip — the linear
readout ceiling on GELU (−120,10) was ~0.5.

The fix is the **signed (polarity) split** (`SignedMBENeuron`): split the input
at x=0 into `relu(x)` and `relu(−x)` magnitudes, each encoded by its own MBE
bank, combined by one readout. This drops the representation ceiling from ~0.5 to
**~3e-5**, and with the closed-form readout the trained MSE reaches **1.3e-4…1.7e-4**
— the paper's regime. This is exactly the signed-magnitude decomposition
[`PASN_method.md`](PASN_method.md) §6 requires, and it is applied identically
inside every PASN bank.

The earlier difficulty remains informative for the project: **a single global
MBE bank fails on GELU precisely where PASN's range-specialized banks are meant
to help** — a range-specialized bank can dedicate resolution to the critical
near-zero
range that one global bank cannot cover while also spanning the wide tail.

## 5. Scope and honest caveats

What is reproduced is the **CPU-verifiable function-approximation layer** of the
paper (Table X, Table VII, the decay ablation). This is achieved with an
implementation that is faithful to the MBE *dynamics* (Eq. 4–8) but adds
mechanisms the paper leaves unspecified (per-basis learnable `α_v`, curvature
calibration, DC bias, closed-form readout, signed split). The paper's exact
fixed-`α`, 5N-parameter neuron trained with only its stated recipe was **not**
separately shown to hit these numbers. The paper's headline results — the full
Transformer conversion framework (spiking FP-mult, Softmax, LayerNorm) and the
ImageNet / NLU / NLG benchmarks (Tables 1–4) — are **not** covered here and
require the GPU phases (planned for vast.ai).
