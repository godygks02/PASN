## 1. What the project is

Training-free ANN→SNN conversion. Each scalar nonlinearity in a Transformer is
approximated by a spiking neuron. The baseline is the **MBE neuron** (Wang et al.,
AAAI-26): one global multi-basis exponential-decay neuron per nonlinearity.

**MBE-PASN** (`src/mbe/mbe_pasn.py`) is our method: read the IEEE-754 sign+exponent bits
to route each input into a *binade* bank, and run **only that bank**. Hard top-1 routing,
no learned gate, zero spikes spent on routing. `R = 1` reduces bit-identically to MBE.

Three neuron variants exist in the tree. **Only `mbe_pasn` is the paper.** `pasn` (SAR)
and `mbe_pasn_s` (shared basis) are kept as ablations — do not develop them.

## 2. Where we are

Experiments 1–11 (1-D function approximation → toy Transformer) and gates P0.0–P0.3 are
done. P0.4 is this document. P0.5 (hardware-honest cost model) follows.

### The headline, stated correctly

On a toy 2-layer Transformer, against a **matched** global-MBE frontier, at
iso-forward-error, 3 seeds:

| stage | spikes vs matched global | stored params |
|---|---|---|
| routing only | **3.94×** | 1812 |
| + per-bank (N, T) | 4.37× | 1893 |
| + signed near-zero | 4.46× | 1907 |
| + order-2 decoder | **5.93×** | 1680 |
| + relative-error identity | 4.82× (**0.81×**, a loss) | 1554 |
| + tied identity | 4.83× (1.00×) | **794** |
| + β | **4.92×** | 1076 |

Global MBE frontier best: 1.65e-2 error @ 1486 spikes, 757 params.

**Numbers you must not quote:** an earlier "9.3×" and "14.2×" were measured against an
unmatched baseline and/or with a double-counting instrumentation bug. Both are dead.
**4.92× is the number**, and it is `iso-forward-error` on a toy — not task accuracy.

### What each piece actually buys

* **Routing is the contribution** (3.94× alone). The paper's centre of gravity is right.
* **order-2 decoder** is the second-largest single step (1.33×) and costs *zero* extra
  spikes — it only widens the readout (`f = Σ w_n o_n + v_n o_n²`).
* **tied identity buys memory, not energy** — exactly 1.00× on spikes (it is an *exact*
  factorisation, so it cannot change the approximation) and 1.96× on stored parameters.
  Never present it as an energy result.
* **β and the signed identity domain do not carry to a network.** Measured, off by
  default, code retained. See §6.

## 3. The three open decisions P0.4 must settle

This is the *point* of P0.4. Everything else in the method is fixed.

### 3.1 `pasn_id_target`: `"relative"` vs `"absolute"` — **the important one**

The spike-driven FP multiply reconstructs both operands through an `MBE_Id`. Two
measurements disagree about how that identity should be budgeted:

* **Experiment 10** (the product itself): with an *absolute* MSE budget a global `MBE_Id`
  lands at **15–49 % relative error** on a real activation×activation product. Small
  operands are essentially not reconstructed. A relative budget fixes this (0.15–0.55 %).
* **P0.3** (the network): switching the routed identity to a relative budget **costs**
  0.81× on spikes at iso-forward-error. A forward error is an *absolute* aggregate, so
  resolution spent on small operands does not pay for itself.

Both are true. **Only Δperplexity can say which matters.** Run both.

Current default is `"relative"`, chosen before P0.3. Do not treat that as settled.

> **⚠️ Run this comparison with `--no-pasn-id-tied`, or it does not run at all.**
>
> `pasn_id_tied=True` is the default (exp 4) and a tied identity **cannot express an
> absolute budget**. The tied path fits one prototype on the *unit* residual and hands
> every magnitude bank that prototype scaled by the routed key, so the relative error is
> flat across banks by construction; an absolute budget means each bank held to the same
> MSE, which for a scale-σ bank is a relative target ∝ 1/σ — a different `(N, T)` per
> bank, which one shared prototype cannot represent.
>
> This was silent until 2026-07-28: the tied branch read `target_rel` unconditionally, so
> `target="absolute"` fell through to a relative budget and built a **bit-identical**
> neuron. The first P0.4 attempt's two headline arms came back equal to the last digit —
> `pasn-both-abs` and `pasn-both-rel-1e-2` both at ppl 21.695, 6.195e5 spikes/token, 8792
> params, 76116 bytes. `build_mbe_pasn` now raises on the combination instead.
>
> It stayed hidden because the waterfall applies *relative identity* (stage 5) **before**
> *tied identity* (stage 6): at the stage where `target` was measured, `tied` was still
> `False`, and once tying went on the knob became a no-op. That is also why stage 6
> measured exactly 1.00× on spikes.
>
> Untied, the two targets really do differ — on the smoke model, absolute 3613
> spikes/token at 1187 params against relative 6629 at 857. Fewer spikes for more
> storage: a real trade, and the one ΔPPL has to settle.
>
> Note this makes §3.1 and exp 4 **one decision, not two**. `tied` is not free here; it
> forces the budget. Report it as such.

### 3.2 `pasn_id_target_rel` (the operating point)

One knob moves the whole network. On the toy, `r` 1e-2 → 3e-2 dropped LayerNorm spikes
131 → 30 and matmul 69 → 16. Sweep it, do not pick one.

### 3.3 Router depth `pasn_e_min` / `pasn_id_e_min` (−3 / −6)

Chosen on the toy's activation distribution. GPT-2's is different — at minimum confirm
they are not obviously wrong; ideally sweep `pasn_id_e_min ∈ {−4, −6, −8}` once.

| stride | `gpt2-medium` | `gpt2` |
|---|---|---|
| **1024 (now the default)** | **21.71** | **29.94** |
| 512 (old default) | 18.46 | — |
| 256 | 18.08 | — |

Table 3 gives 22.76 / 29.41 and the MBE paper 22.34, so stride 1024 is within 2.8 % of
target on medium, and `gpt2` independently landing on 29.41 to 1.8 % is what confirms the

Two consequences. ΔPPL is a ratio against our own ANN at the same stride, so the P0.4
comparison was never at risk — what was at risk is any sentence putting our absolute

### 4.1 Scope separation

GPT-2 Stage 1 converts **MLP GELU + every LayerNorm** only. HF computes QK^T / softmax /
attn·V functionally, so there is no module to hook — attention stays exact FP. Run three
scopes so the LayerNorm identity's contribution is separable:

| scope | why |
|---|---|
| activation only | the part experiments 1–2–6 improved |
| LayerNorm only | where the identity (60–70 % of toy spikes) lives |
| both | the number to report |

`gpt2_wikitext.py` already has an `only = {...}` selector near line 251 — check how it is

### 4.2 The matrix

```
backend    ∈ {mbe (matched baseline), mbe_pasn}
scope      ∈ {act, ln, both}
id_target  ∈ {relative, absolute}          # §3.1 -- the decision
r          ∈ {3e-3, 1e-2, 3e-2}            # relative only
```

The `mbe` baseline must be **matched**, exactly as in P0.3: give it
`mbe_readout_order=2` and `mbe_id_logsample=True`, and sweep `n_basis_*` so it gets its

> **⚠️ `mbe_readout_order` reaches LayerNorm but NOT the activation.** GELU crosses zero
> and is non-monotone, so `build_activation` auto-selects the polarity-split
> `SignedMBENeuron` — and `make_signed` does not take `readout_order`, nor does
> `signed.py` mention it anywhere. There *is* no order-2 readout for the signed neuron;
> `_fit_signed_activation` passes the argument nowhere and says so in a parenthetical.
> Verified 2026-07-28: order 1 and order 2 build a bit-identical signed activation
> (fingerprint 19.523948072, 85 params both), while the unsigned path (37 → 43 params)
> and LayerNorm's rsqrt (49 → 57) both honour it.
>
> So every `mbe-*-matched` row in `results/gpt2_p04.json` is matched on LayerNorm and
> **order-1 on the activation**, which is 58–67 % of that backend's spikes and exactly
> where PASN's order-2 decoder was the second-largest single win (1.33×, free). This is
> trap 3 again, walked into while trying to honour trap 3. **Say so wherever those rows
> are quoted.**
>
> It is not, however, a likely explanation of the divergence below: the gap is 28–594×
> in perplexity, not 1.33×, and the recorded failure mode of our global MBE is the
> near-zero GELU error floor (~0.07), which a decoder order does not lift. The frontier
> supports that — a4-l6 → a6-l8 cut the error 21× while spikes moved 2 %, so what is
> missing there is representational, not energetic. Closing the gap properly means
> giving `SignedMBENeuron` an order-2 joint readout and re-running the frontier; that was
> deliberately **not** done mid-matrix.

### 4.3 The runner exposes the knobs (done 2026-07-28)

`experiments/gpt2_wikitext.py` now passes the decision knobs through to `ConvertConfig`:
`--pasn-id-target`, `--pasn-id-target-rel`, `--pasn-id-e-min`, `--pasn-readout-order`
for the routed side, and `--mbe-readout-order`, `--mbe-id-logsample`, `--n-basis-ln`
for the matched baseline. CLI defaults are read from `cv.ConvertConfig()` at import, so
an unset flag means "the audited default" and cannot drift from `audit_defaults.py`.

`--json PATH` appends one record per run to a JSON array (`--tag` labels it), and
`--backend none` writes an ANN-only row. Sequential runs only — it is a
read-modify-write, so parallel runs need separate paths and a merge.

Knobs that do not reach the chosen backend are stored as `null`, not as their default:
`_build_one` branches on `backend in _ROUTED_BACKENDS`, so `pasn_*` is dead for `mbe`
and `mbe_*` / `n_basis_*` is dead for `mbe_pasn`. A column that varies while nothing
changes is trap 6 one level up, in the analysis rather than the sweep.

Verified on `--smoke`: all four arms build, the two `id_target` values give different
spike counts and different stored bytes, `audit_defaults.py` passes, 33 tests pass.
Smoke perplexity is from random weights — it says the wiring is live, nothing more.

### 4.4 Success criteria

Against the **matched** global MBE, not against the paper's table:

* **≥2× fewer spikes at equal perplexity**
* **ΔPPL ≤ 1 %** vs the ANN at some point on the frontier
* per-layer error does not compound with depth (check layer-wise, GPT-2 is 24 layers —
  our own MBE reimplementation historically failed exactly here)

Only compare to the paper's `+0.35 %` after attention is converted too (Stage 2). Saying
otherwise is comparing a partial conversion to a full one.

## 5. Method state — what is decided, and where it lives

`ConvertConfig` (`src/mbe/convert.py`) is the authority. `audit_defaults.py` checks it.

| setting | value | from |
|---|---|---|
| `pasn_budget` | `"rule"` | exp 1 — per-bank `(N,T)` from `b = log2(Δ/√ε)` |
| `pasn_near0` | `"signed"` | exp 2 — no bank straddles zero |
| `pasn_readout_order` | `2` | exp 6 — free accuracy, zero extra spikes |
| `pasn_id_tied` | `True` | exp 4 — homogeneous target ⇒ one prototype |
| `pasn_alpha_init` | `"auto"` | exp 1 |
| `pasn_beta` | `{}` (off) | P0.2 — measured, does not carry |
| `pasn_id_signed` | `False` | P0.2 — halves invocations, costs 15 % spikes |
| `spike_lambda` | `0.0` | exp 8/9 — only helps below the `T = 2` floor |
| `learn_tau` | `True` | exp 6/9 — frozen kernels are the accuracy corner, not this one |

**Known inconsistency (deliberate, do not "fix" mid-run):** `build_mbe_pasn`'s *own*
defaults are still the neutral primitives (`budget="fixed"`, `target="absolute"`,
`tied=False`, `readout_order=1`) while `near0` is already `"signed"`. The conversion path
overrides all of them, so GPT-2 is unaffected. Unifying them would silently change the
results of `experiments/*.py` scripts that rely on defaults — do it *after* the GPU work,
not before.

## 6. Traps already stepped in — do not repeat

1. **Amdahl, twice.** A per-primitive speedup means nothing until multiplied by that
   primitive's share. The activation was 8–17 % of toy spikes, so experiments 1–2's 3.8–8×
   became ~1.1× overall. Then β was 13.6× on `inv` and 1.02× on the network, because
   `inv` is the *small* part of Softmax (which is `exp2` + `inv` + identity, identity
   dominating). **Always check the share before celebrating.**
2. **Instrumentation asymmetry.** `spiking_cost_report` patches both `forward` and
   `reconstruct`; routed neurons implement `reconstruct` *as* `self.forward`, so one call
   was charged twice while a plain `MBENeuron` was charged once. Fixed (per-neuron depth
   counter, `test_cost_report_counts_one_call_once` pins it). If you add a neuron type
   whose `reconstruct` delegates, the test covers you — keep it passing.
3. **Unmatched baselines.** Giving the routed side every improvement while the baseline
   keeps old defaults inflated the headline 3×. Match, then compare.
4. **Metric must fit the quantity.** Element-wise relative error on `Wx` gave nonsense
   (139, 2.75) because `Wx` crosses zero by *cancellation* — absolute error does not
   shrink with the result. Use `‖err‖/‖exact‖` for sums, element-wise relative for
   products.
5. **Control groups decide the answer.** The firing-rate penalty looked 5× *worse* until
   its arm was allowed the same `T` values as the baseline arm; properly matched it is
   neutral above ~4 spikes and opens a new low-energy corner below the `T=2` floor.
6. **Sweep a knob the stage responds to.** In the first P0.3 attempt, stages holding the
   identity at an absolute target were swept over `pasn_id_target_rel` — three identical
   configs — which would have failed the "routing alone" criterion for the wrong reason.
7. **`num_learnable()` is not bytes.** `learn_tau=False` moves the τ's to buffers, which
   *look* like a 45 % memory saving and are not one (they are still stored). Any memory
   claim needs real state-dict bytes — that is P0.5.
8. **Process parallelism, not threads.** The tensors are tiny; 22 cores gave a 1.24×
   thread speedup. Ten single-threaded processes beat it ~4×. Irrelevant on GPU for the
   *eval*, but the primitive **fitting is CPU** and dominates conversion wall time.
9. **A knob that is accepted is not a knob that is applied.** Two of P0.4's own settings
   were silently dropped by the code they were passed to, and both were found only by
   diffing the built neuron rather than by reading the call site.
   * `pasn_id_target="absolute"` did nothing under the default `pasn_id_tied=True`
     (§3.1) — the two headline arms came back bit-identical.
   * `mbe_readout_order=2` does nothing for the GELU activation (§4.2) — the signed
     neuron has no order-2 readout to give.

   In both cases the argument was in the signature, accepted without complaint, and
   ignored. **Before trusting any sweep, build both arms and compare the state dicts** —
   equal fingerprints mean the knob is dead. `build_mbe_pasn` now raises for the first
   case; the second is still silent.
10. **Two metrics of the same quantity that disagree in sign — check the de-duplication
    rule before believing either.** P0.4 reported PASN with *fewer* learnable params
    (8792 vs 9249) and *twice* the bytes (76116 vs 38148). The bytes were the bug:
    `neuron_params` goes through `parameters()`, which de-duplicates shared tensors by
    object, while the byte sum walked `state_dict().values()`, which lists a shared
    tensor once per key referencing it. `TiedBank` holds one prototype by reference and
    `fill_unreachable` points unreachable banks at a fitted neighbour, so the routed
    backend was charged for sharing it does not pay for — and the global MBE, which
    shares nothing, was measured exactly. **The measurement was not symmetric, so only
    the comparison broke.** Fixed in P0.5 (`storage_bytes` de-duplicates by storage and
    keeps buffers); real numbers are 35168 vs 38148, i.e. PASN 1.08x *smaller*. Note the
    lesson cuts both ways: trap 7 correctly said `num_learnable()` is not bytes, and the
    replacement metric introduced a fresh one-sided bias. Apply the same rule to both
    arms and check that the side with no sharing is unchanged.

  ```bash

  cd pasn && pip install -r requirements-vastai.txt
  ```

  consider `--epochs` lower for the sweep and full for the final points.
